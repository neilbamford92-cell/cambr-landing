Privacy Policy

Last updated: 7 November 2026

This policy covers the Cambr app on iPhone and Android, and cambr.uk. Using Cambr is also covered by our Terms of Use.

Cambr is a driving app that scores roads for driving quality and guides you along the best ones. You can also share drives, photos and reviews with other drivers, follow them, and drive together in a convoy. To do that Cambr needs your location, and it keeps a record of the drives, roads and posts you choose to save. This page explains what we collect, why, who else sees it, and how to delete it.

2A. Speed and location: what can be seen and shared

On your screen. While you drive, Cambr shows your speed next to the speed limit. Your phone works it out from GPS. Speed limits and speed cameras come from data stored inside the app, so showing them sends nothing about you to us.

In a saved drive. We do not store your speed. A saved drive keeps its route as a line of positions with no time attached to any point, plus when it started and ended, its distance and its duration. Nobody, including us, can tell from a saved drive how fast you went on any road or at any moment.

What other people can work out. For a drive you share, other people see its distance, its duration and when it started, so they could work out your average speed over the whole drive, stops included. Nothing in Cambr shows a top speed, a speed on any road, or where you were at a given time.

Drive totals. The distance and duration of your saved drives also count toward one combined “miles driven with Cambr” figure, never the route.

Road tags. When you tap “Tag this road”, the tag keeps your speed and direction at that moment and about 60 seconds of positions with their times. Only you can see your tags. We use them to improve road scores.

Diagnostics. The drive log on your phone records your speed at moments of a drive (for example every 30 seconds, and each time it re-plans your route) and rough positions. It stays on your phone unless you send it to us.

Directions. When Cambr asks Mapbox for a route while you are moving, it sends your position and the direction you are heading, not your speed.

Convoys. Cambr never sends your speed to a convoy. It sends your position, the direction you are heading and how far along the shared route you are (section 5A).

Who sees your speed. No one but you, and us when you send us your diagnostics or tag a road. We do not sell or share it, and we do not give it to insurers or anyone else unless the law requires us to.

These companies process data for us, only to run Cambr:

  • Supabase: our database, sign-in, photo storage, server functions and the private channel convoys use. The database and photo storage are hosted in London.
  • Mapbox: maps, place search, directions and map pictures. It receives the area of map you look at, what you search for (with your approximate location, to find nearby places), your position, heading, stops and destination when it plans or re-plans a route, and route lines when it draws a picture of a drive. We have switched off Mapbox’s own usage data collection.
  • Google: Sign in with Google; spoken directions on iPhone (Google’s text-to-speech receives the words of each instruction from our server, which can include road and place names, but not your location or your account); push notifications on Android; Google Play testing; email to hello@cambr.uk; and Google Analytics on the website only (section 4).

  • Expo: passes push notifications to Apple and Google. It receives your push token and the notification’s words, for example “jess_t commented on your drive” and up to 140 characters of the comment.
  • Resend: sends the sign-in emails.
  • Kit: our mailing list. We add the email address on your Cambr account to Kit so we can send you emails about Cambr and its beta, and we add the emails of beta testers invited through Apple TestFlight or Google Play testing. 5. What other users can see

Your profile. If you choose a username, your profile is public: anyone can see your username, bio, profile picture, when you joined, your drive stats (the number of drives, miles and roads by tier), the cars you choose to show, your gallery photos, your featured drive and the drives you share. Signed-in drivers can also see who you follow and who follows you. Without a username you appear as “Driver” and a number. Your email and the name from Apple or Google are never shown.

Your drives. When you save a drive you choose who can see it: Everyone (the public Feed and your profile), Followers (people who follow you) or Private (only you). It starts on Everyone, then remembers your last choice. You can change it later. Anyone signed in can follow you without asking, so Followers means anyone who has chosen to follow you. You can remove a follower or block them at any time.

For a drive other people can see, they see its title, note, mood, photos and car, the date and the times it started and ended, its distance and duration, the scored roads you drove, and its route line with the ends hidden:

  • the first and last mile, plus an extra distance of up to a quarter of a mile that is different for every drive and never shown. It is measured as a circle round where you started and finished, so it also hides a loop that comes back past your start;
  • 300 m round your saved home wherever the drive passes it, and the first and last mile round your home if the drive starts or ends within half a mile of it;
  • if what is left is shorter than a mile, no line at all (“Route not shown”).

You always see your whole drive. Changing a drive’s visibility, or setting or moving your home, hides the line again.

If someone drives a route you shared or saves roads from your drive, you may be told, with their username, and the same goes for you on someone else’s drive.

Comments, likes and posts. Comments and replies can be seen by anyone who can see what they are on. Likes show who liked something. Feed posts (gallery photos, photos of cars on your profile, and posts you make in the Feed) are public. A post that tags a road can also be shown on that road’s page.

Reviews are public, with your username.

Only you (and we) can see the roads you tag while driving, your saved roads and routes, your home, cars you do not show, route searches, feedback and diagnostics.

You can delete a drive, post, photo or comment at any time. To remove a review, email hello@cambr.uk.

5A. Convoys

When you join a convoy, you agree to share your position with its other members while you drive together; the join screen says so.

What is sent: your position (to about a metre) and the direction you are heading, so each car shows on the map; how far along the convoy’s shared route you are (to the nearest 10 m); whether you are off the route; and the time. Never your speed.

When: only while the convoy drive is running and you are driving it. It stops when you leave, the convoy ends, you are removed, you sign out or you end your drive. Your position is never sent within 300 m of your saved home or within 400 m of where you set off, and none is sent at all until Cambr has checked whether you have a saved home and knows where you set off. In those places the others see your car as hidden.

Who: only the members of that convoy, over a private channel that nobody else can join. We do not store live positions.

What we keep: the convoy’s name, code, shared route (with 300 m round the host’s home removed and 400 m cut off each end), who joined, roles, car labels, and when people joined and left. A convoy ends when the host ends it, or 12 hours after it starts (24 hours if it never starts).

5B. Notifications

Cambr tells you, in the app, when someone likes or comments on your drive or post, replies to or likes your comment, follows you, drives your route or saves roads from your drive. If you allow push notifications (signed-in accounts only), we store your phone’s push token and send these as pushes, but not between 10pm and 7am. Push messages show the other driver’s username and up to 140 characters of their comment or your drive’s title. You can turn each kind off in Settings → Notifications. Messages “From Cambr” are off unless you turn them on. Notifications are kept for 90 days; a push token is deleted when you sign out, delete your account, or after 180 days unused.

5C. Reports, blocks and moderation

You can report a drive, post, photo, comment, review, profile picture, car or driver, and block a driver. A report hides the item for you straight away. When two signed-in drivers report the same thing, it is hidden for everyone except the person who posted it until we have looked. We aim to look at every report within 24 hours. We remove content that breaks the Community guidelines, and we can suspend or close accounts. Usernames, bios and comments also pass an automatic word filter.

A driver you block is not told. You stop seeing each other’s drives, reviews, comments and profile, any follow between you ends, and neither of you can join a convoy the other is already in.

We keep a report with the account that made it, and a copy in our feedback inbox naming the account reported. Both go when either account is deleted.

6. How long we keep it

For as long as you have an account, apart from:

  • notifications: 90 days after their last activity;
  • push tokens: until you sign out or delete your account, or 180 days unused;
  • convoy live positions: never stored. A convoy’s record stays after it ends, until the host deletes their account; your own place in it goes when you delete yours;
  • wrong convoy codes: about a day;
  • our server logs: about 7 days.

Deleting your account (section 7) removes your account and everything linked to it, immediately and permanently. Backups are kept for up to 30 days.

7. Deleting your account

In the app: Profile → Settings → Delete account. This removes straight away: your account and sign-in, your profile (username, bio and picture), drives with their full lines and photos, gallery photos and Feed posts, saved roads and routes, your home, garage and car photos, reviews, comments and replies, likes, follows (both ways), blocks and reports you made, road tags, feedback, route searches, shares, drive totals, notifications (and your name in other people’s), push tokens, and convoys you host or joined. If you signed in with Apple, Cambr is also disconnected from your Apple ID. Feedback you sent us may also have been copied into our feedback tracker, and your email may be on our mailing list: email hello@cambr.uk and we will remove both.

Without the app: go to cambr.uk/delete-account, or email hello@cambr.uk from the address on your account.

If you have a Cambr Pro subscription, deleting your account does not cancel it: cancel it in the App Store or Google Play first.

Backups are cleared within 30 days.

8. Your rights

Under UK GDPR you can ask us to access, correct, export or delete your personal data, or object to or restrict how we use it. Deletion is built into the app, and available without it at cambr.uk/delete-account. For anything else email hello@cambr.uk and we will respond within one month. Where we rely on your consent you can withdraw it at any time: location in your phone’s settings, push notifications in your phone’s settings or Settings → Notifications, and the newsletter with the unsubscribe link in any email. You can also complain to the Information Commissioner’s Office at ico.org.uk.

9. Location

Cambr uses your precise location, after you allow it, to show roads near you, guide and record drives, and share your position in a convoy you join. During a drive you have started, Cambr keeps receiving your location while the screen is off. On iPhone you will see the blue location indicator; at your first drive Cambr may ask whether to allow location “Always”, and saying no does not stop the drive recording. On Android you will see a “Cambr is recording your drive” notification the whole time, and Cambr does not ask for Android’s background location permission. It all stops when you end the drive. Cambr never uses your location in the background outside a drive you have started.

10. Children

Cambr is for drivers aged 18 and over. We do not knowingly collect data from anyone younger. If you think a child has an account, email hello@cambr.uk and we will delete it.

11. Security

Data is encrypted in transit and at rest. Every record is tied to your account and protected by access rules in the database, so other users can read only what you choose to share (section 5) and can never change it. Profile pictures and car photos are stored at hard-to-guess web addresses that anyone with the link can open. Drive photos and gallery photos are stored privately and open only for people who can see them. The app never contains server credentials.

12. Changes

We will update this page when Cambr’s data use changes and note the date at the top. If a change affects how your data is used or who can see it, we will also tell you in the app before it takes effect.
