The Evo Triangle, leg by leg

Three roads across the Denbigh Moors, used as a test route for the best part of thirty years. What each leg is actually like, which way round to drive it, and whether it still earns the reputation.

Neil Bamford · 12 August 2026 · 8 min read

The Evo Triangle is the most famous stretch of road in Britain that is not a single road. It is three of them, joined into a rough triangle across the empty moorland south of Denbigh: the A543, the B4501 and a leg of the A5. It got the name because Evo magazine used it, a lot, for the sort of testing that needs long sight lines and no traffic.

When we measured every road in Britain, all three legs came out in A-tier. That is a useful result, because it says something specific: no individual leg of the Triangle is one of the very best roads in the country. What is exceptional is the combination.

The three legs

  1. A-tierA54326 km

    The long leg

    The one people mean when they say the Triangle. Climbing out of Pentrefoelas onto bare Denbigh moorland, it opens into long, fast, sweeping curves with sight lines measured in hundreds of metres. There is very little to hit and very little that will surprise you, which is precisely why testers liked it. It is also the leg where the enforcement is, and where the reputation has caused the most trouble.

    Averages around 33 mph in measured conditions, which is high for moorland but tells you it is a flowing road rather than a technical one.

  2. A-tierB450115 km

    The interesting leg

    The one most people underrate. Narrower than the A543 and considerably more involving, threading past the Llyn Brenig reservoir with well-linked bends that actually ask something of you. If you are driving the loop for enjoyment rather than for a top speed run, this is the best fifteen kilometres of it.

  3. A-tierA512 km

    The connecting leg

    Fast, open, high-speed curves along Telford's Holyhead road, and the least distinctive part of the loop taken on its own. Averages nearly 39 mph, the quickest of the three. Its job is to close the triangle, and it does it well.

No single leg is one of Britain's best roads. Thirty-three miles of them joined into a loop is a different proposition.

Which way round

Anticlockwise, starting at Pentrefoelas, is the usual answer and the better one. It puts the A543 climb first while you are fresh and while the road is opening up in front of you rather than dropping away, saves the more technical B4501 for the middle, and returns you along the A5 to where you started with the option of going round again.

Clockwise works, and the A543 descent towards Pentrefoelas is genuinely good, but the B4501 is better approached from the Denbigh Moor end.

Where to start, and where to stop

Pentrefoelas is the practical corner: it is on the A5, it has fuel and food, and it is about forty minutes from the A55 at Conwy. Cerrigydrudion is the alternative and is marginally quieter. There is nothing at all on the A543 or the B4501 themselves, which is part of the appeal but does mean planning fuel before rather than during.

Sportsman's Arms sits at the high point of the A543 and is the traditional stop, though check opening before relying on it. The Llyn Brenig visitor centre on the B4501 is the other option and has parking with a view.

The part that needs saying

The Triangle's fame has been a problem for it. Average speed cameras have been installed on sections of the A543 after sustained concern about speeding and collisions, and the roads see regular patrols. There has been real local frustration about drivers treating a public road across farmland as a circuit, and the coverage that made these roads famous is part of why.

None of that makes it a worse drive. It is a magnificent piece of road and it does not need illegal speed to be one. The A543's sight lines mean you can carry good pace legally and see everything coming, which is the actual pleasure of it. The B4501 is more fun at fifty than the A543 is at ninety.

Sheep are unfenced across the entire moorland section, including on the A543 where the sight lines encourage you to relax. That is the genuine hazard here, not the corners.

Is it worth the trip?

On its own, from a distance, probably not. Thirty-three miles is under an hour, and if you have driven six hours to get there you will want more than that.

As part of a north Wales day, absolutely. The Triangle sits within half an hour of the B4391 across the Migneint, which came out in our top tier and which is, on the numbers and in our view, a better road than any single leg of the Triangle. Bala is forty minutes south. The Horseshoe Pass is half an hour east. Build a day around all of it and the Triangle becomes the fast, open counterpoint to a set of tighter mountain roads, which is a much better use of it than laps.

Common questions

What is the Evo Triangle?

Three roads across the Denbigh Moors in north Wales, forming a rough triangle: the A543 between Pentrefoelas and Denbigh, the B4501 past Llyn Brenig, and the A5 between Cerrigydrudion and Pentrefoelas. The name comes from Evo magazine, which used the roads regularly for testing.

How long does it take?

About fifty minutes for a lap at legal speeds, covering roughly thirty-three miles.

Is it a private circuit?

No. These are ordinary public roads with farms, junctions, cyclists, walkers and unfenced livestock on them. The name is a nickname, not a designation.

Is it camera enforced?

Average speed cameras have been installed on sections of the A543, and the area is patrolled.
