The best driving roads in Wales, measured

Three roads in Wales have reached the top of our scale, and fifteen more are worth going out of your way for. Here they are north to south, with how to string them together.

Neil Bamford · 12 August 2026 · 11 min read

Wales is the most efficient driving country in Britain. Not the most spectacular, which is Scotland, and not the one with the most great road in it, which is northern England. But for great road per hour of driving, nowhere else comes close. You can cross the country in three hours and take in eight roads that would headline a weekend anywhere else.

It is also the part of Britain the motoring press has covered most thoroughly, which is useful: when we measured Welsh roads and set the result against what Evo, Autocar, Top Gear, Goodwood and the rest have written, the two lined up more closely here than almost anywhere.

What follows is every Welsh road currently in our top two tiers. S-tier is the top of the scale. A-tier means the road is worth a detour on its own account.

North Wales and Snowdonia

  1. S-tierB4391 · Bala to Llangynog24 km

    B4391 across the Berwyns

    The best road in Wales, and the one most likely to be new to you. Open sweeping bends across high moorland build to a genuinely dramatic descent off the Berwyn range towards Llangynog. It is beautifully paced: fast enough to flow, sighted well enough to commit, and quiet enough that you rarely have to back off for anything.

    The sort of road Autocar reaches for when there is something special to test.

  2. S-tierB4391 · Blaenau Ffestiniog to Bala13 km

    B4391 across the Migneint

    Same road number, separate stretch, and it scores separately because it drives as its own thing. This is the wilder half, running across the bare Migneint moors between Ffestiniog and Bala. Fast, open, superbly sighted and almost always empty. Driven end to end with the Llangynog section it makes close to forty kilometres of continuously excellent road, which is as good as anything in Britain.

  3. A-tierA4086 · Capel Curig to Llanberis18 km

    A4086 Llanberis Pass

    Running directly beneath the highest peaks in Wales, this is the postcard Snowdonia drive: open sweeps with a raw mountain wall on either side. It is a cornerstone of any serious North Wales trip, though in high season you will share it. Go early.

  4. A-tierBetween Llanuwchllyn and Dinas Mawddwy12 km

    Bwlch-y-Groes

    The second-highest public road in Wales. A steep, narrow climb over remote moorland with gradients that ask real questions of the car. Single-track in places, which is part of the challenge rather than a complaint, but pick your moment and your vehicle.

  5. A-tierA542 · Llangollen to Ruthin12 km

    A542 Horseshoe Pass

    Short, well-known and consistently good. The climb out of Llangollen opens into a wide sweeping hairpin section with the Ponderosa café at the top, which is either the point or an obstacle depending on how busy it is.

  6. A-tierA498 · Beddgelert to Pen-y-Gwryd13 km

    A498 Nantgwynant

    Flowing curves past Llyn Gwynant beneath the high Snowdonia peaks. Less demanding than the Llanberis Pass and more consistently enjoyable, and the natural other half of a Snowdonia loop.

The Denbigh Moors

Three roads across the Denbigh Moors form the loop that has been used as a test route for decades. All three are in A-tier and they are best driven as a set, so they have their own guide.

  1. A-tierA543 · Pentrefoelas to Denbigh26 km

    A543

    The long leg. Fast, open and sweeping across bare moorland, with the sight lines and rhythm that made this network a benchmark for road testers in the first place.

  2. A-tierB4501 · Denbigh Moor to Cerrigydrudion15 km

    B4501

    The tighter leg, threading past Llyn Brenig reservoir with flowing, well-linked bends and almost no traffic.

  3. A-tierA5 · Cerrigydrudion to Pentrefoelas12 km

    A5

    The quick leg that closes the loop. Open, high-speed curves, and the least interesting of the three on its own, but it is what makes the other two into a circuit.

A full guide to the loop, including where to start it and why it works as a test route, is in the Evo Triangle guide.

Mid Wales and the Cambrian Mountains

This is the emptiest driving in Wales and the least written about. If you want roads where you can genuinely settle into a rhythm without meeting anything, come here rather than Snowdonia.

  1. A-tierRhayader to Devil's Bridge23 km

    Elan Valley Road

    Threading past the chain of Victorian dams and reservoirs, then climbing out onto open mountain. Flowing rather than fast, and among the most consistently scenic driving in the country. Averages around 23 mph, so treat it as a road to enjoy rather than press on.

  2. A-tierA44 · Llangurig to Aberystwyth28 km

    A44 to the coast

    A grand mid-Wales mountain road climbing west from Llangurig towards the sea. The final third, dropping towards Aberystwyth, is the best of it: fluid, smooth and quick.

  3. A-tierBeulah to Tregaron

    Abergwesyn Pass

    A wild single-track crossing of the Cambrian Mountains, culminating in the hairpin sequence known as the Devil's Staircase. Remote, narrow and genuinely rugged. This is a road you drive for what it is rather than how fast you can go, and it is not the place to meet a wide vehicle in a hurry.

  4. A-tierB4574 · Cwmystwyth to Devil's Bridge9 km

    B4574

    A twisting mountain road through old mining country, near-continuously turning with barely a straight in it. Short, but there is almost nothing wasted.

South Wales and the Brecon Beacons

  1. S-tierA4069 · Llangadog to Brynamman25 km

    A4069 Black Mountain Pass

    The best-known driving road in Wales and one of the few that lives up to the coverage. A fast, sweeping climb over the western Beacons opening onto wide moorland, with long sight lines and corners you can commit to properly. Exposed at the top and prone to weather, and busy on a good Sunday.

  2. A-tierB4560 · Beaufort to Llangynidr17 km

    B4560 Llangynidr moors

    A cracking mountain road climbing onto the moors above Crickhowell, crammed with hairpins, switchbacks and camber changes. It is a natural test route and it has been used as one, by Top Gear and Classic and Sports Car among others.

  3. A-tierA4067 · Brynamman north29 km

    A4067

    Runs parallel to the A4069 up the northern side of the Black Mountain, and is the faster of the two: high-speed sweeps and wide moorland views. If the A4069 is busy, this is the answer rather than the compromise.

  4. A-tierA4059 · Hirwaun to Storey Arms14 km

    A4059

    A fast open climb into the central Beacons beneath Pen y Fan, sweeping bends and big mountain views. Averages around 42 mph, one of the quickest roads on this list.

  5. A-tierB4235 · Chepstow to Usk17 km

    B4235

    A swooping, empty B-road through rolling farmland. Not a mountain road and not trying to be, but if you are coming over the Severn it is a far better first twenty minutes in Wales than the A449.

Wales rewards linking roads more than any other part of Britain. Almost nothing here is more than an hour from the next good thing.

Three ways to link them

A North Wales day

Start at Bala. Take the B4391 west across the Migneint to Blaenau Ffestiniog, come back over it, and continue east on the Llangynog section. That is close to forty kilometres of top-tier road before lunch. Add the Denbigh Moors loop in the afternoon and the Horseshoe Pass on the way out.

A Snowdonia day

Capel Curig to Llanberis on the A4086, down to Beddgelert, back through Nantgwynant on the A498. It is a natural circuit and it takes in the two best Snowdonia roads with a proper mountain backdrop the whole way. Bwlch-y-Groes is an hour south if you want to finish somewhere quieter.

A Brecon day

The A4069 south over the Black Mountain, back north on the A4067, then east across to the B4560 above Crickhowell. Three A and S-tier roads inside sixty miles, and Abergwesyn is within reach if you want to finish somewhere genuinely remote.

A note on when to go

Most of these are moorland or mountain roads, which means three things. They close in snow, and the A4069, the Black Mountain and the higher Cambrian roads are among the first to go. They are busiest on summer Sundays, particularly the A4086 and the A4069. And several of them, particularly Bwlch-y-Groes and Abergwesyn, are narrow enough that meeting a motorhome mid-climb is a real event.

Early morning, out of season, is when Wales is at its best. It is also when you will have the B4391 entirely to yourself, which is the single strongest argument for getting up early anywhere in Britain.
