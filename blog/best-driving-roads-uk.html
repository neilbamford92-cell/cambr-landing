Britain / The top tier

The best driving roads in the UK, measured

Every road in Britain, ranked on the nine things that make a drive worth taking. Twenty-three made the top tier. Some you will know. Several you almost certainly will not.

Neil Bamford · 12 August 2026 · 9 min read

Every list of Britain's best driving roads is somebody's opinion. Usually a good opinion, held by someone who has driven a lot of roads. But an opinion all the same, shaped by which roads that writer happened to be sent to in a press car, and which ones a photographer could get a decent shot of.

We wanted to do it a different way. So we took every road in the UK and measured them: how tight and how frequent the corners are, how much the road climbs and falls, how wide it is, what the surface is like, what the traffic does to it, how enforced it is, and what the landscape around it is doing. Nine things in total. Then we set that measurement against what the motoring press has actually written, from Evo, Autocar, Top Gear, Hagerty, Goodwood and more than twenty other sources.

The result is a map with three tiers on it. B-tier roads are good, and worth grabbing when you are already nearby. A-tier roads are worth going out of your way for. S-tier is the top of the scale, and as of this month twenty-three roads in Britain and Ireland have reached it.

This is the S-tier list, region by region, with what each road is actually like. If you want the full picture including every A-tier road near you, that is what the map is for.

The top of the scale is not where the famous roads are. It is where the roads that reward driving are.

South West England

  1. S-tierA39 · Porlock to Lynton40 km

    A39 Exmoor

    From the 1-in-4 descent of Porlock Hill to the cliffs above Lynton, this is as complete a piece of road as England has. Steep gradients, tight corners and long open moorland runs, most of it within sight of the sea. It comes out top of our entire database, and it is not close.

    Top Gear has called it as complete a piece of driving as you will find in England. Few who have driven it disagree.

  2. S-tierB3306 · St Ives to St Just22 km

    B3306 West Cornwall

    Cornwall's Atlantic coast in road form. Tight, walled and exposed, with an average speed of about 23 mph. This is not a fast road and it does not want to be one. It scores where it does because of how relentlessly it turns and what it turns through.

    A connoisseur's road rather than a quick one. Picked out by Top Gear among the country's finest.

Northern England

Northern England holds more S-tier road than anywhere else in Britain: seven in the north of England proper and two more across the North Pennines. If you want the highest concentration of great driving within a single weekend, this is where it is.

  1. S-tierA686 · Penrith to Alston37 km

    A686 Hartside Pass

    The corners come thick and fast on this Pennine climb, building through blind, tightening bends to a summit with views clear across to the Lakes. A fixture of best-driving-road features, and an easy one to see why.

  2. S-tierNorth York Moors9 km

    Blakey Ridge

    Riding the spine of the North York Moors, this high open road flows fast and uninterrupted for miles, with sight lines that let you read every bend ahead. Nine kilometres is short for an S-tier road. It earns the place on quality per mile rather than distance.

  3. S-tierWensleydale to Swaledale10 km

    Buttertubs Pass

    A sharp, dramatic crossing between two Dales, all sudden climbs, crests and tight technical sections between high fells. Long regarded across the motoring press as one of the great Yorkshire roads.

  4. S-tierA537 · Macclesfield to Buxton12 km

    A537 Cat and Fiddle

    One of Britain's most famous and most demanding moorland roads. A relentless sequence of sweeping bends and shifting cambers across the Peak District tops. Heavily enforced, and rightly so given its casualty record, but as a piece of road design it remains a benchmark.

  5. S-tierB6255 · Ingleton to Hawes27 km

    B6255 Ribblehead

    A wide, fast Dales B-road past the Ribblehead viaduct and Three Peaks country, trading tight corners for big open sweeps and enormous moorland views. One of the highest average speeds of any road in the top tier, which tells you what kind of road it is.

  6. S-tierA57 · Manchester to Sheffield28 km

    A57 Snake Pass

    The best known of the trans-Pennine routes, climbing over bleak high moorland with long exposed sweeps, big elevation changes and a reputation for closing at the first hint of snow.

  7. S-tierB6278 · Consett to Middleton-in-Teesdale47 km

    B6278 North Pennines

    Almost nobody writes about this one, which is exactly why it is here. Forty-seven kilometres of fast, wide, well-surfaced B-road striking across the high North Pennines, with sweeping moorland miles and big elevation swings. The longest road in the top tier.

  8. S-tierB6295 · Cowshill to Allenheads25 km

    B6295 Weardale to Allendale

    The other North Pennines road most lists miss. Empty, high and consistently engaging, in a part of the country where you can drive for half an hour without meeting anything.

  9. S-tierB6276 · Middleton-in-Teesdale to Brough22 km

    B6276 Teesdale to Brough

    A high crossing of the Pennines linking Teesdale to the Eden valley, and the third of the three North Pennines B-roads that between them make this the most underrated driving region in England.

Three of the twenty-three are North Pennines B-roads that rarely appear on anyone's list. That is the single most useful thing measurement does. It finds the roads nobody was sent to in a press car.

Wales

  1. S-tierB4391 · Bala to Llangynog24 km

    B4391 Berwyn crossing

    Open sweeping bends across Welsh moorland giving way to a dramatic descent off the Berwyn range. Confidence-inspiring and beautifully paced, and the sort of road Autocar reaches for when there is something special to test.

  2. S-tierA4069 · Llangadog to Brynamman25 km

    A4069 Black Mountain Pass

    A fast, sweeping climb over the western Brecon Beacons, opening onto wide moorland with long sight lines and flowing, committed corners.

  3. S-tierB4391 · Blaenau to Bala13 km

    B4391 Migneint moors

    The B4391's second, wilder half, running across the Migneint. Fast, open, beautifully sighted and near-deserted. It scores separately from its Bala to Llangynog sister because it runs as a separate stretch, and it earns the tier on the same character.

Scotland

  1. S-tierA821 · Aberfoyle to Callander19 km

    A821 Duke's Pass

    Compact but relentless. This Trossachs climb packs in a density of turns that shames far longer roads, and it opens the door to Scotland's great driving country.

  2. S-tierA82 · Rannoch Moor to Glencoe43 km

    A82 Glencoe

    Fast and flowing beneath Scotland's most storied peaks. The run through Glencoe pairs serious pace with scenery almost no road on earth can match. As much pilgrimage as drive.

Ireland

  1. S-tierR115 · Wicklow Mountains

    R115 Military Road and the Sally Gap

    The old military road across the Wicklow Mountains, running through bog and open mountain with barely a building on it for miles.

  2. S-tierR574 · Beara Peninsula

    R574 Healy Pass

    A stacked switchback climb over the spine of the Beara Peninsula, and the closest thing Ireland has to an Alpine pass.

What did not make it, and why

Some of the roads people expect to see here are not in the top tier, and the reasons are worth being straight about.

Bealach na Bà sits just below, in A-tier. It is the most Alpine road in Britain and a genuine bucket-list drive, but a single-track climb with an average speed around 22 mph is being rated for spectacle rather than driving flow. Cheddar Gorge is the same story: a magnificent set-piece, permanently full of traffic. Winnats Pass is one of the most photographed roads in the country and comes out as B-tier, because it is 1.4 km long, does not turn much, and averages 22 mph in tourist season.

That gap between how a road photographs and how it drives is the whole reason we built this. A road can be the most beautiful thing you see all year and still not be much of a drive. Both of those things can be true at once.

How to actually drive them

Very few of these roads are worth a special trip on their own. Blakey Ridge is nine kilometres. Buttertubs is ten. What makes them worth the drive is linking them, and the geography is generous about it: Buttertubs, Blakey Ridge, the B6255 and the three North Pennines B-roads are all comfortably inside one long weekend from a base at Hawes or Barnard Castle. Snake Pass and the Cat and Fiddle sit within an hour of each other. The two Welsh B4391 stretches are the same road, driven end to end.

The rest of the map is where most of the value is. Twenty-three roads is a list. Several thousand A and B-tier roads is a way of driving.
